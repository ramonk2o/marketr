# Channel: meta-ads

Paid ads on Facebook and Instagram (plus Threads, Messenger, WhatsApp and Audience Network), bought in Meta Ads Manager.

Verified: 2026-11-01

## Pieces and fields

Every number below is a Meta **recommendation** from the Ads Guide unless marked "hard". The Ads Guide publishes no hard maximum for primary text or headline: Ads Manager accepts longer text and truncates it. Write to the recommendation, because that is what people see.

**Placements.** The Ads Guide lists Facebook Feed, Facebook in-stream video, Facebook Marketplace, Facebook Stories, Facebook search results, Facebook Business Explore, Facebook Reels, Ads on Facebook Reels, Facebook Groups Feed, Facebook right column, Instagram Feed, Instagram Stories, Instagram Explore home, Instagram Reels, Messenger Stories, and Audience Network (native, banner, interstitial, rewarded video). Threads became an Ads Manager placement in January 2026 and is on by default in Advantage+ placements (widely reported). WhatsApp Status ads are rolling out. Messenger inbox is no longer listed.

- Single image and video: primary text, headline, description, CTA button.

## What to deliver

- 3-5 primary texts, each a different angle (problem, benefit, proof, offer, objection) and length: one that fits before the cut (under ~125 characters), one medium, one long for audiences that read.
- 3-5 headlines that each stand alone. If Facebook Feed is among the placements (it is in Advantage+ placements), write them at 27 characters or fewer; 40 is fine only when the ad runs on Instagram, Marketplace or Stories alone.
- 1-2 descriptions of up to 30 characters, written as optional: they often do not show.
- The CTA button chosen from Meta's list, with the reason.
- A text overlay for image, Story and Reel creatives: 3-7 words, inside the safe zone.
- For video: the first-3-seconds hook (on-screen text and spoken line) and captions.
- For carousels: per card, a headline (20) and an optional description (18).
- For lead ads: intro headline, intro bullets or benefits, the questions, completion headline, description and button text.
- Placement notes: which text is the short version for Reels and Stories.
- The Special Ad Category, if one applies, so the media buyer knows targeting will be limited.

## Best practices

- Put the whole message in the first ~125 characters: most people never tap "See more", so what sits after the cut is a bonus, not the pitch.
- Lead with the benefit or hook, not the brand name: the Page name and avatar already show who is speaking.
- Write each text option so it works alone and next to any headline. With multiple text options (up to 5 per field, widely reported) Meta mixes options per person, and with "optimise text per person" it may show primary text as a headline or the other way round. A headline that only makes sense after one particular primary text will meet the wrong one.
- Make the options differ in angle, not just wording: Meta can only learn which angle wins if there is a difference to learn.
- Do not repeat the CTA button in the headline ("Shop now" button + "Shop now!" headline): it wastes the most visible line.
- Keep text on images short. Older Meta guidance said images with under 20% text perform better; the hard 20% limit is gone, but the reason stands: the image has to read in the half-second of a scroll, and the copy has its own fields.
- Keep overlays out of the top ~14%, the bottom ~35% and ~6% on each side of 9:16 creatives: profile name, caption, CTA and buttons cover those areas. This applies to Stories and Reels, and also to 9:16 ads in Facebook and Instagram Feed and Facebook in-stream reels. On Reels ads that carry a disclaimer (finance, health), leave the bottom 40% clear, because the disclaimer takes more space. For 1:1 and 4:5 in Instagram Feed, keep the bottom and side edges clear.
- Video: hook in the first 3 seconds with motion or a question on screen, since that is when people decide to stop scrolling. Design for sound off and add captions (Meta: "optional, but recommended"): many people watch muted.
- Lead form: ask only what sales will use and say in the intro what happens next: every question costs completions, and unclear forms bring poor leads.
- Messaging ads: write the greeting as the question the person would ask, and the ice breakers as the 2-4 most common real questions: it removes the effort of typing the first message.
- Match the landing page: the offer, price and wording the ad promises have to be on the page it opens, both for conversion and because mismatches get ads rejected.
- Ask which Advantage+ creative enhancements are on. Meta lists Translations (AI), Text generation (AI), Text improvements (AI) and Enhance CTA (AI), some on by default; they can rewrite or add to the copy the client approved. For claims-sensitive clients, recommend reviewing or turning them off. Generative features may be unavailable for special ad categories, finance and health.

## Policies and restrictions

Ads are reviewed against Meta's Advertising Standards before and while they run. A rejection loses delivery time and repeated violations restrict the ad account, so it is cheaper to write within them.

**Personal attributes.** Ads cannot assert or imply, directly or indirectly, a personal attribute of the viewer or of their family: race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), financial hardship, voting status, trade union membership, criminal record, or name. The test: does the copy say or hint "you are X"? Talk about the product or the group, not about the person reading. "Meet seniors" is allowed; "Meet other seniors" is not, because "other" implies the reader is one.

| Not allowed | Allowed rewrite |
|---|---|
| "Are you overweight?" | "A meal plan built for steady weight loss" |
| "Do you have diabetes?" | "Diabetes-friendly recipes, every week" |
| "Are you bankrupt? Check out our services." | "Debt advice from licensed counsellors" |
| "Ready to upgrade your skin to look younger?" | "A retinol night cream for smoother-looking skin" |
| "Meet other Buddhists" | "Buddhist meditation groups near you" |
| "Are you 18 years old?" | "Open to adults 18 and over" |

Meta's own examples: "Do you have diabetes?", "Meet other Buddhists" and "Are you a convicted felon?" are not allowed; "Bulimia counseling available" and "Meet seniors" are. Second-person questions, "your [condition]" and "other [group]" are the usual traps.

**Health and weight loss.** Weight-loss, dietary and cosmetic-procedure ads must target people 18+. No statements of inferiority about appearance, body parts or hygiene; no close-ups pinching fat; no claim of results from wearables alone; supplements must state the time to see results; no clickbait in health or weight contexts. No claim to cure, heal or eliminate incurable diseases (diabetes, cancer, HIV, Alzheimer's); managing symptoms is allowed. Before/after images are allowed for general cosmetic products and procedures. For weight loss the policy has no specific before/after rule, but as a writer's caution avoid them: the body-image rules make them easy to reject. Prescription drugs need Meta's written permission.

**Financial and insurance.** Credit cards, loans and insurance must target 18+ and carry the disclosures the law requires (Meta does not list them; check local law). Ads cannot ask for financial details. Payday loans, loans of 90 days or less, binary options, CFDs and ICOs are prohibited. Some products need licence verification. No guaranteed investment returns (Prohibited Commercial Practices).

**Special ad categories.** Ads about financial products and services (including credit), employment, housing, or social issues, elections or politics must be declared. For the first three: no gender choice, age fixed to 18-65+ (credit in Europe may use a different range), no lookalikes, no demographic or behaviour targeting, location by radius of at least 15 mi / 25 km (15 km in Europe), no ZIP or postcode targeting, no location exclusion. Political ads are declared too, but their audience options are not limited by the label. Because the audience will be broad, the copy cannot rely on "for women in their 30s" or similar; a job or housing ad that names who may apply can also break the Discriminatory Practices policy.

**Social issues, elections, politics.** Requires authorisation and a "Paid for by" disclaimer, plus disclosure of photorealistic AI-made or edited images or video. Not allowed in the European Union since October 2025, with a narrow exception for election-authority information.

**Misleading claims.** Deceptive pricing, unauthorised endorsements (a celebrity or another brand without permission) and guaranteed returns are prohibited. Every number, price and "free" must be true and on the landing page.

**Restricted categories.** Alcohol, dating, gambling, tobacco, weapons, drugs, crypto, addiction treatment and others need targeting limits, authorisation or both; check the Advertising Standards for the client's category before writing.

**Profanity, shocking and sensational content.** Profanity, violent or graphic content and clickbait (especially in health) get rejected; write curiosity, not alarm.

**Branded content.** When a creator is paid or given products, the post needs the paid-partnership label with the brand tagged. Disclosure law still applies on top of the label.

## Language notes

- CTA button text is fixed: you pick from Meta's list (for example Learn more, Shop now, Sign up, Book now, Contact us, Get quote, Subscribe, Download, Apply now, Get offer, Order now, Call now, Send message, Send WhatsApp message). You cannot write your own, and Meta shows it in each viewer's interface language. Do not translate or restate it in the copy.
- The recommendations were set for English. As a rule of thumb, German, French, Portuguese and Spanish run 15-30% longer, so aim at the low end of each range and check the 27-character headline.
- The "See more" cut depends on lines on screen, so ~125 is only a guide: a line break, emoji or wide script uses the space faster. Preview in Ads Manager.
- Hashtags work on Instagram but use up the visible part of the text; put them at the end, if at all.
- If "Translations (AI)" is on, Meta may show machine-translated copy to other-language audiences; for markets that matter, write the translation yourself.

## Pitfalls

- A headline that repeats the first line of the primary text: Meta may show them together, and the ad says the same thing twice.
- The offer or price after the "See more" cut: most people never see it.
- Text options that depend on each other ("As we said above..."): Meta combines them freely.
- A CTA only in the headline or button on an Awareness video in Facebook Feed: the footer does not show on mobile.
- A carousel whose cards only make sense in order: people swipe from any card, and Meta can reorder them when that option is on.
- Overlays at the bottom of a Story or Reel: covered by the caption and CTA.
- "You" plus a condition, "other [group]", or a question about the reader's body, money or identity: rejected under personal attributes.
- Lead-form questions about health, money, identity or beliefs: prohibited by the Lead Ads policy.
- "Guaranteed" results, "free" with hidden conditions, or a price that differs from the landing page.
- A social-issue angle (environment, health policy, immigration) in a normal brand ad: it can be classified as a social issue ad, which needs authorisation and cannot run in the EU.
- Assuming the description shows: on most placements it does not.

## Example

Good (Facebook Feed, gym chain):
> 7 days free, no card needed. Classes from 6am, showers included, 2 minutes from Central Station.

Why: offer, condition and the two strongest reasons fit before the cut (~95 characters), there is no personal attribute, and it reads fine above any headline.

Bad:
> Are you tired of feeling out of shape? At FitCo, our passion has always been helping people like you transform their bodies and their lives with...

Why: "Are you tired of feeling out of shape?" implies the viewer's physical condition (personal attributes, likely rejected), the brand's passion takes the visible space, and the offer never appears before "See more".
