# Channel: video

Short vertical organic video: Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts (not ads).

Verified: 2026-11-01 (sources at the end)

## What to deliver

Three different texts, because they do different jobs:
- **Spoken script:** what the presenter or voice-over says. Written to be heard: short sentences, spoken rhythm.
- **On-screen text:** a few words per beat that carry the message with the sound off. Not a transcript; captions or subtitles do that.
- **Caption / title:** what appears under the video: a hook line, the topic in searchable words, the CTA, hashtags.

Plus: cover text (3 to 6 words), duration target, and subtitle note (auto captions on or burned in).

Script format:

| Time | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | "Stop kneading your dough like this." | Stop doing this | Close-up of hands kneading wrong |
| 2-8 s | ... | ... | ... |
| 25-30 s | "Save this for your next bake." | Save for later | Finished loaf |

For videos with no speech, leave Spoken empty and add audio notes in Visual.

Timing check: count words in Spoken and divide by the speech rate. English runs about 2.5 words per second (~150 words per minute, observed common rate for presenters); a 30 s video holds about 75 spoken words, less with pauses and visual beats.

## Format agreement

Agree once per client and save in `channels.md`. When the client has videos, watch or read them (captions, transcripts) and propose the pattern you see instead of asking in the abstract.

| Decision | Default options |
|---|---|
| Duration | 7 to 15 s (loops, trends) / 20 to 45 s (tips, offers) / 60 to 90 s (tutorials) / up to 3 min (stories, deep tips) |
| Presenter | Person on camera / voice-over on footage / text only with music |
| Who speaks | Owner, team member, customer, creator partner |
| Subtitles | Platform auto captions / burned-in subtitles in the brand style / none (text-only videos) |
| On-screen text style | Hook only / one line per beat / full key points |
| Caption length | One line plus CTA / short paragraph / long, keyword-rich |
| Hashtags | 0 / 1 to 3 / up to 5 on Instagram (the cap); on YouTube a few (more than 60 voids them all; in the title they eat the 100 characters); same or different per platform |
| CTA | Follow, save, comment a word, link in bio (where the account has one), visit; one per video |
| Cross-posting | Same video on all three / adapted per platform |

## Structures

- **Hook, problem, solution, CTA (20 to 45 s):** state the result or the mistake in the first 2 s, show the problem, show the fix, one CTA. The viewer decides in the first seconds whether to keep watching; every beat after that must pay off the hook.
- **Tutorial (30 to 90 s):** show the end result first, then numbered steps with one on-screen line each, then the result again. Showing the result first answers "is this worth my time?"
- **Behind the scenes (15 to 45 s):** open mid-action, a voice-over or text revealing something people do not usually see, end with an invitation. Curiosity carries it; a slow intro kills it.
- **Myth vs fact / list (15 to 30 s):** "3 things nobody tells you about X", one per beat. Numbers set expectations and keep people until the last item.
- **Loop (7 to 15 s):** the last frame leads back into the first, so a replay feels seamless. Replays add watch time.
- **Testimonial (20 to 40 s):** the customer's result in the first line, their words, the brand at the end. A brand logo first reads as an ad and gets swiped.

## Best practices

- Hook in the first 1 to 3 seconds, in both picture and words: viewers swipe almost instantly, and the hook is the only part everyone sees.
- Say and show the hook at the same time: some watch with sound off, some listen without looking.
- Keep on-screen text out of the bottom ~35%, the top ~14% and the sides, and more on the right for TikTok and Reels buttons: captions, buttons, the username and the CTA cover those areas.
- Turn on captions or burn in subtitles: a large share of people watch muted; text keeps them following.
- Keep on-screen lines short, at most about 6 words per beat, on screen long enough to read twice: people read slower than they listen.
- Cut every pause and intro ("Hi guys, today..."): dead time early loses viewers before the point.
- Write the topic in plain searchable words in the caption and say it aloud: TikTok and Instagram use captions, on-screen text and speech to understand and surface videos (reported).
- One CTA, said and shown near the end: people act on one clear ask; a CTA too early interrupts the payoff.
- Post native, without other platforms' watermarks: Instagram says it shows less of watermarked reels and of reels already posted on Instagram.
- Export in good resolution, with sound and without borders, and avoid reels that are mostly text: Instagram says low-resolution, muted, bordered and majority-text reels are shown less.
- Trends and audio: a trending sound can help reach, but trends expire fast and business accounts may not have the track. Treat as a note for the user, never a dependency of the script.

## Policies and restrictions

- **Branded content:** Meta requires the paid partnership label when a partner gave value (payment, free products, affiliate commission). TikTok requires the commercial content disclosure setting for posts promoting a brand, product or service: choose "Your brand" or "Branded content", and the post shows "Commercial content" or "Paid partnership". TikTok says undisclosed posts may be removed or restricted. YouTube has a "paid promotion" setting for the same purpose (not re-checked here).
- **Music rights:** business accounts get a limited commercial library: TikTok business accounts see only the Commercial Music Library (TikTok); Instagram business accounts are reported to be limited to licensed or Meta Sound Collection tracks. On YouTube, a Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide, so keep claimed music under 60 s or use YouTube's audio library. Do not script around a popular song unless the user confirms the account can use it.
- **Claims:** health, finance, weight loss, before and after and medical claims are restricted by community guidelines on all three platforms; spoken claims count as much as written ones. Check the client's `restrictions`.
- **Country rules:** local ad disclosure (e.g., a spoken or written "ad" or "publi") may be required in addition to the platform label. Check the client's restrictions for the country.
- **Minors and people on camera:** image rights and consent for staff and customers shown; stricter rules for minors in many countries.

## Language notes

- Speech rate differs by language and by person: Spanish, Japanese and Italian pack more syllables per second than English; German words are longer. Time the script with the client's language and, if possible, a read-aloud by the presenter.
- On-screen text expands in translation (often 20 to 35% for German, Portuguese, French versus English, observed): design the lines in the target language, not in English first.
- Subtitles in the client's language; auto captions are less accurate in some languages and for accents, so check them before posting.
- Hashtags and keywords in the language people search in.

## Pitfalls

- Spoken script copied as on-screen text: doubles the reading load and covers the picture.
- A logo or title card in the first second: looks like an ad and gives no reason to stay.
- Text in the bottom third: hidden behind the caption and buttons.
- A script too long for the duration: the presenter rushes or the edit cuts the CTA.
- "Link in bio" on TikTok when the account cannot have a bio link yet, or "link in description" on Shorts: the viewer finds nothing to tap.
- Relying on a trending song the account cannot use: the video is muted or rejected.
- Same caption pasted to all three platforms with "#reels": wrong tag on two of them.

## Example

Good hook (accountant, tutorial):
> Spoken: "If you freelance, you're probably paying tax on money you could deduct."
> On-screen: "Freelancer? You're overpaying"
> Visual: presenter points at a receipt pile.

Why: names who it is for and what they lose in one sentence, said and shown at once, under 3 seconds.

Bad hook:
> Spoken: "Hi everyone, welcome back to our channel! Today we're going to talk about taxes."
> On-screen: logo animation.

Why: three seconds with no reason to stay; the topic is generic and the payoff is not stated.
