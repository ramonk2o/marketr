# Guide: brand.md

Answers "how we speak". Everything a writer needs to sound like the client, short enough to fit on one screen: copy applies concrete moves and ignores long prose. Owned by branding; discovery drafts it from the tone of existing material and what the client says they want, marked as hypotheses.

## Sections

- **Personality:** 3 traits, each as "is / is not / therefore": the trait, its limit, and a concrete writing move ("close, not intimate: name the producer and the farm instead of describing tasting notes"). Adjectives alone fit every brand and guide no writing. Traits say how to speak in any context; what to lead with (price, origin) lives in `positioning.md`, or every piece would open the same way.
- **Signature moves:** 3 or 4 habits of the writing: how a piece opens, how the reader is addressed (você, a gente, tu), sentence length, emoji use.
- **Tone by context:** only the 2 or 3 contexts the client really uses (sales, social, support or bad news), one line each.
- **Vocabulary:** words and expressions to use, and clichés of the category to avoid. Words banned for legal or client reasons live in `company.md` restrictions; claims the brand drops live in `positioning.md`.
- **Told story:** the origin and the why, as the brand tells it, only when a real origin exists. The facts behind it live in `company.md`.
- **Right and wrong examples:** 3 or 4 short pairs, preferably real approved pieces, each from a different context. Keep the best; replace rather than accumulate.

## Does not belong here

What the client stands for strategically (`positioning.md`).

## Example

Good: "Close, not snobbish; therefore talk like someone who knows the producer, not a sommelier. Right: 'This lot came from Seu Antônio's farm, picked in July.' Wrong: 'Notes of red fruits with a velvety body and persistent finish.'"

Bad: "Tone: professional and friendly." (Fits every brand; guides no writing.)
