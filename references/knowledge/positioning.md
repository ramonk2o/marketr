# Guide: positioning.md

Answers "why us". The strategic choice of how the client wants to be seen, against the alternatives. Owned by branding; discovery drafts it only from clear statements by the client. It is a decision, not a finding: it stays `[hypothesis: team]` until the client approves it through a `strategy` page.

## Sections

- **Positioning statement:** one sentence, in this shape: "For [segment] who [situation], [brand] is the [category] that [difference], because [fact]." Use the category the customer already searches for; inventing one costs an education budget small clients do not have. The "because" fact proves the difference in the same sentence, not another strength. The sentence doubles as the elevator pitch.
- **Main alternative:** what the customer would do instead, in one line, often not a competitor (buying single bags at the supermarket, doing it themselves, doing nothing), and the difference against it. Copy that only compares with other brands misses the choice most customers actually make.
- **We are / we are not:** 1 to 3 contrasts ("close, not gourmet snob"), each telling copy what to avoid. The customers the client does not want more of often say what the brand is not.
- **We don't say:** claims and clichés the brand drops ("gourmet", "the best coffee").
- **Message pillars:** 2 or 3 (up to 5 for distinct products). A category requirement every competitor meets is a proof point under a pillar, not a pillar of its own. Each pillar is a theme with a pointer to the fact behind it (`company.md`, `product.md`) and the `icp.md` objection it answers, by name. Point; do not restate: the facts keep their own evidence and markers where they live.
- **Lead pillar by segment:** one line per segment in `icp.md`: the segment's name, the pillar to lead with, and the objection to answer first. Awareness level stays in `icp.md`.

The difference does not have to be unique. When nothing sharper holds, it is the obvious choice executed well, stated through 2 or 3 concrete, checkable executions ("roasted the week it ships; the producer's name on every pack"). Swap test: if the sentence still reads true with a competitor's name in it, rewrite it with the client's own facts.

A pillar never promises what does not exist ("most awarded" with no awards): copy repeats pillars in every piece.

Product level (`products/<slug>/positioning.md`) only when the product has its own `icp.md` or `competitors.md`.

## Does not belong here

Product attributes and specs (`product.md`), evidence about customers (`icp.md`), how to say it (`brand.md`).

## Example

Good: "For people who want a better coffee at home without becoming experts, Serra Alta is the specialty coffee subscription that brings each lot's producer story to your door, because we roast the week it ships and buy direct from 12 Mantiqueira producers (company.md)."

Bad: "Quality, tradition and innovation." (Any competitor could say it; fails the swap test.)
