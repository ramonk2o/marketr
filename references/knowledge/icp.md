# Guide: icp.md

Answers "who buys". The customer as they are, in their own words whenever possible. Owned by research; discovery drafts it from what the client and the team believe, so most of a first draft is `[hypothesis: team]` or `[hypothesis: client]`.

## Sections

- **Segments:** the distinct groups that buy, defined by situation and job ("gives coffee as a gift", "remote worker replacing the coffee-shop habit"), each with a short name reused across files. Demographics only describe a segment; they do not define one. Products point to these names. When segments of one product differ from another's, use `products/<slug>/icp.md`.
- **Decision maker and influencers:** who decides, who pays, who uses, who influences, when they differ.
- **Pains:** problems and frustrations the customer feels, as they would describe them.
- **Desires:** what they want to achieve or feel.
- **Triggers:** moments and events that make them start looking, and what they used before.
- **Objections:** doubts and resistances, in the customer's words, split into before buying (questions prospects ask) and after buying (disappointments in reviews and cancellations). No answers here: answers live in `product.md`.
- **Awareness level:** per segment, how aware the typical customer is of the problem and of solutions like the client's (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware), with what it rests on (the words they search, whether they mention a previous solution).
- **Where they are:** channels, media, communities, places where they can be reached.
- **Literal language:** real quotes with source and date, grouped by use: pain, desire, objection, outcome, the words they use for the product or category, words they never use. The most valuable section for copy; never invent it. Keep the best 10 or so; the rest stay in the research collection in `_in/`, which `sources` lists.

## Evidence

After research, pains, desires, triggers, objections and segments carry their support in short form: `(strong: 7 people, 3 sources)`, `(moderate: 3 people, 1 source)`, `(weak: 1 comment)`. Copywrite leans on strong items and treats weak ones as hypotheses. Whose voice it is matters too: say when an item comes only from competitors' customers or from prospects.

## Does not belong here

What the product does or answers to objections (`product.md`), why the client is the right choice (`positioning.md`).

## Example

Good: "Pains: 'I spend a fortune at the coffee shop and the coffee at home is never as good.' (Google review, 2026-08) Remote workers who drink 3+ cups a day. (moderate: 4 people, 2 sources)"

Bad: "Target: men and women aged 25 to 55, classes A and B." (Demographics only; no pain, desire or language a skill can write from.)
